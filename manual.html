Start drawing

There are five ways in, and you can switch between them at any time. All of them end up as the same thing: a stack of simple shapes you can click and change.

  1. Draw along. Step-by-step guides show a faint picture to trace, like tracing paper, and tick off each part as you place it. Start with the butterfly, snowflake or flower (about three minutes each).
  2. Draw with the pen. Press P and draw. Wobbles are smoothed out, and if you hold still at the end of a rough circle, box or triangle, it snaps into a clean one.
  3. Color it in. Coloring pages: pick a color, click a part, done.
  4. Start from a picture. Open one of thirteen finished drawings (butterfly, ghost, snowflake, mandala, kitty and more) and change anything.
  5. Write code. Describe a drawing as instructions for a pen, like repeat 5 [ move 200 turn 144 ] for a star. See Drawing with code.

Nothing is ever lost by trying. Every change is one step in History (bottom right). Click any step to go back to it, or press Ctrl+Z. Your drawing is also saved in your browser automatically.

The studio at a glance

Shelf (left)
Five tabs: Blocks (shapes to add), Draw along (guides and coloring pages), Pictures (finished drawings to start from), Code (drawing instructions) and Helper (suggestions; the number badge counts them).
Tool rail
Select V, Pen P, Eraser E, Stamp S, Color picker I and Paint bucket K. The bar above the canvas shows the options of the tool in use.
Canvas (middle)
Click to select, drag to move, scroll to resize, Shift+scroll to rotate. Pink dashed lines are smart guides: parts snap to centers and edges (hold Alt to stop snapping). The small monitor shows how many blocks there are, how many shapes they become after repeats, and how fast the picture draws.
Settings (right)
Everything about the selected part: place, shape, color, outline, effects, repeats and clipping. With nothing selected, it shows the canvas: size (square, poster, card, desktop or phone wallpaper) and background.
Layers
Top of the list is drawn on top. Drag to reorder (or Alt+↑/↓), click the eye to hide, the lock to stop accidental moves.
History
Every step you took, with a name. Click one to jump back (or forward) to it.

Blocks

A block is one simple part: a shape, a line, an algorithm or some text. Most drawings are 5 to 15 blocks stacked on top of each other.

GroupBlocksGood for
ShapesCircle, Box, Triangle, Polygon, Star, Heart, Petal / leaf, Drop, Egg, Moon, Ring, Wing, Ghost, Blob, CloudBodies, heads, petals, wings, clouds. Nearly everything.
LinesStraight line, Curve, Arc, Wave, Zigzag, Spiral, Pen strokeStems, whiskers, antennae, hair, borders.
AlgorithmsFractal tree, Snowflake arm, Koch snowflake, Sierpinski triangle, Seed spiral, Sunburst, Pattern fillDetail that would take hours by hand: branches, crystals, sunflower seeds, textures.
CharactersText, Cute faceTitles, cards, and instant faces for anything.

Every block has the same core settings: position, size, rotation, flip, fill (solid or gradient), outline, see-through amount, and an effect: drop shadow, glow, sticker (white edge), soft focus or long shadow. Clip inside another block hides everything outside a chosen shape, which is how you put spots on a wing without spilling over the edge.

Shading adds light and shadow to one part in one click (from the Helper or the command palette), and a soft ground shadow so things stop floating.

Repeats

Repeats are the trick that makes simple drawings look rich: one block, many copies, arranged by a rule.

RepeatWhat it doesTry it on
MirrorAdds a flipped copy, like folding paper. Left and right, top and bottom, or all four corners.Butterflies, faces, hearts, ears
Spin aroundCopies spun evenly around a center. Kaleidoscope mode mirrors each copy too.Snowflakes, flowers, suns, mandalas
Step and repeatEach copy moves, turns and shrinks a little more than the last.Tree tiers, trails, stacks
GridRows and columns of copies.Wallpaper, tiles, fabric, polka dots
ScatterSprinkles copies at random (the same every time, unless you change the seed).Stars, snow, confetti, falling leaves
Golden spiralCopies placed by turning a fixed angle each time; 137.5° packs them like sunflower seeds.Sunflowers, pinecones, galaxies

Repeats stack: a Mirror on top of a Step makes two rows of wing spots. A safety limit stops any one block from making more than a few thousand copies, so the studio never freezes. Press M to add or remove a Mirror on the selected block.

The pen and brushes

Press P (or pick Pen on the tool rail). The pen draws real, editable strokes: after you draw, each stroke is a block you can recolor, move or smooth.

  • Brushes: Ink (even line), Brush (thick and thin, by speed or pen pressure), Marker (see-through), Pencil (sketchy), Neon (glowing) and Dots.
  • Symmetry: draw once, get a mirror, a 4- or 6-way spin, or a 6-, 8- or 12-way kaleidoscope. Mandalas take a minute.
  • Steady hand: smooths out shakes as you draw. Turn it up for calm curves, down for lively lines.
  • Shape snapping: draw a rough circle, box, triangle or star and hold still for a moment; it becomes a clean shape block. Curves and scribbles are never forced into a shape.
  • Close loops: a stroke that ends where it started can fill with color.
  • Stamp (S): click or drag to scatter stars, sparkles, hearts, dots and flowers, with optional rainbow colors.
  • Eraser (E) removes pen strokes (and code drawings) you touch; switch it to Everything to erase picture parts too. Trace photo lays a picture under the canvas to draw over (it is never saved or exported).

Pens, tablets and touch screens all work. Two fingers pinch to zoom, and a resting palm is ignored.

Color

  • Palettes: Candy, Pastel, Forest, Ocean, Sunset, Autumn, Winter and Ink. Click a swatch for the first color, Shift+click for the second (gradients).
  • Shuffle colors recolors the whole picture with a random palette. Undo if you liked it better before.
  • Harmonies: recolor from one color and keep every light and dark in place: analogous (neighbors), complementary (opposites), triadic (three-way), split complementary, or monochrome.
  • Paint bucket (K): click a part to fill it; drag across it for a gradient in that direction.
  • Color picker (I): click anything to take its color. Recently used colors are kept for you.

Rule of thumb: pick one dark, one light, one or two middles and a single bright accent. The Learn page explains why.

The Helper

The Helper looks over your shoulder like an art teacher. It checks for things artists learn to look for, and offers a one-click fix for most of them:

  • Composition: start with one big main shape; make it bigger when it is lost on the page; give it room to breathe at the edges; nudge parts that are almost (but not quite) centered; bring back parts that slipped off the page.
  • Color: a main part that blends into the background; more colors than a tidy palette needs.
  • Lines and spacing: outlines of different weights; two parts that almost touch (touch or separate them, so it looks on purpose); shapes that are almost a mirror image.
  • Depth: add shading, put things on the ground with a soft shadow, try a background, and make faces cuter.

Hover a fix to preview it, click to apply. Every fix is a single step you can undo, and the Helper also tells you what is already working, like strong contrast or nice symmetry. You stay the artist.

Drawing with code

The Code tab lets you draw by writing instructions for a pen, the way the Logo language has taught people to code since 1967. It is the fastest way to make patterns that would take hours by hand, and everything it makes becomes normal, editable blocks.

The pen starts in the middle of the page, facing up. move draws a line forward; turn turns clockwise by some degrees. Change a number and the preview updates as you type. Click Add to drawing (or press Ctrl+Enter) to keep it.

// a square: four sides, four right angles
size 6
repeat 4 [
  move 200
  turn 90
]

Repeating with a counter

Inside a repeat, the name i counts 1, 2, 3… Use it to make each step a little different.

color blue  size 3
repeat 80 [
  move i * 4
  turn 89
]

Color, brushes and the page

color sets the line color, fill the inside of shapes. Use a name (pink, gold, navy…), a code like #ff4f87, or hue(…) for rainbow colors. brush neon makes glowing lines; background navy black blends the page from top to bottom.

background navy black
brush neon  size 5
repeat 48 [
  color hue(i * 7.5)
  move 300
  back 300
  turn 7.5
]

Shapes at the pen

circle, oval, box, star, heart, petal, polygon, dot and write place real shape blocks where the pen is, turned the way it faces. Use penup or jump to move without drawing, and goto x y to go straight to a spot (0 0 is the middle; y grows upward).

penup
repeat 6 [
  set row i
  repeat 6 [
    goto (i - 3.5) * 110 (row - 3.5) * 110
    fill hue((i + row) * 30)
    heading (i + row) * 15
    star 40 5
  ]
]

Symmetry

symmetry mirror, symmetry spin 6 and symmetry kaleido 8 copy everything you draw next, using the same repeats as the rest of the studio.

background ink black
symmetry kaleido 8
brush neon  size 4
repeat 30 [
  color hue(180 + i * 4)
  move 10 + i
  turn 25 - i
]

Randomness you can repeat

random(a, b) picks a number between a and b. The same seed always gives the same picture, so you can share code and get identical results; change the seed for a new arrangement.

seed 3
background sky cream
penup
repeat 60 [
  goto random(-360, 360) random(-360, 360)
  fill hue(random(180, 260), 80, 70)
  dot random(4, 22)
]

Make your own instructions

define teaches the studio a new instruction. Give it a name and the inputs it needs; afterwards you use it exactly like a built-in one. This is how small instructions grow into a whole toolkit of your own.

// define NAME INPUTS [ what it does ]
define square len [
  repeat 4 [ move len  turn 90 ]
]

// now "square" works like any other instruction
size 4
repeat 12 [
  color hue(i * 30)
  square 220
  turn 30
]

Instructions that use themselves

An instruction can call itself with smaller numbers. Add an if that stops it, and you get branching trees, ferns and snowflakes: the same idea as the Fractal tree and Koch blocks. push remembers where the pen is and pop jumps back there, which is perfect for branches.

define branch len [
  if len < 12 [ stop ]
  size len / 10
  move len
  push  turn -25  branch len * 0.72  pop
  push  turn 20   branch len * 0.68  pop
]
color #6b4423
jump -330
branch 170

My instructions: your personal library

Click Save to My instructions to keep every define in the editor. Saved instructions work in every drawing from then on, appear as buttons you can click to insert, and can be edited, downloaded as a text file or removed. Saving one with the same name replaces the old version. The library is stored in your browser (and in the desktop app on your computer).

The library starts with two examples, ngon sides len and flower petals len, so you can see the idea before writing your own.

Rules for names: letters, numbers, _ and dashes between letters (petal-ring is fine). A name cannot be a built-in instruction; the studio suggests another (like my-move). Inputs are numbers.

Safety limits

Code can never freeze the studio. A program stops after 250,000 steps or 120,000 points, an instruction can call itself 80 levels deep, and a repeat goes up to 100,000 times. Whatever was drawn before a limit still shows in the preview, with a message saying what happened.

When something is wrong

Mistakes come back as plain messages with the line number highlighted, such as I don’t know the instruction “trun”. Did you mean turn? or The “[” to repeat (line 3) is never closed with “]”. Two details catch most people:

  • A minus sign touching a number starts a new number: goto 0 -250 is two numbers. To subtract, put spaces on both sides: len - 1, or write it joined to a name: len-1.
  • Angles are in degrees, and turn is clockwise. 360 is a full turn.

Command palette

Press Ctrl+K (⌘+K on a Mac) or click Search in the top bar. Type a few letters of anything (“star”, “export svg”, “mirror”, “theme light”, “snowflake”) and press Enter. The palette lists every tool, every block, every starter picture and draw-along guide, the code examples, alignment and grouping for the current selection, export formats and themes, each with its shortcut so you learn them as you go. Letters can be out of word order; exsvg finds “Export SVG”.

Copy, paste and tracing

Ctrl+C copies the selected parts as text, so you can paste them into another drawing, another browser tab, the desktop app, or even send them in a message and paste them back later. Pasting puts the copy slightly down and to the right, keeps groups together, and selects it so you can move it right away. Pasting a whole project file’s text adds all of its parts.

Paste a photo (for example, a screenshot), or drag an image file onto the canvas, to lay it under the canvas for tracing with the pen. It is never saved or exported. Only trace photos you took or that are public domain.

The canvas can be square, a 3:4 poster, a 5:7 card, a 4:3 landscape, a 16:9 desktop wallpaper or a 9:16 phone wallpaper (with nothing selected, see Canvas settings).

Desktop apps

artful drawing also comes as an app for Windows, macOS and Linux. It is the same studio, works fully offline, opens in its own window with normal menus (File, Edit, View), and asks where to save exports like any other program. Get it from the latest release:

The warnings appear because the apps are not code-signed (signing certificates cost money each year). The full source is public, and the apps are built by GitHub from that source, so you can check exactly what is inside.

Put it on your own website

artful drawing is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build step, so any static host works. To run your own copy on GitHub Pages from an Ubuntu terminal:

gh repo fork Normansrule/artful-drawing --clone
cd artful-drawing
gh api -X POST "repos/{owner}/{repo}/pages" -f "source[branch]=main" -f "source[path]=/"
gh api "repos/{owner}/{repo}/pages" --jq .html_url

To try it on your own computer without publishing: python3 -m http.server 8000 in the folder, then open http://localhost:8000. The README has every command, including building the desktop apps yourself.

Privacy

Your drawings, code and saved instructions stay on your device. There are no accounts, no tracking, no ads and no uploads. Share links carry the picture inside the link itself. The website loads fonts from Google Fonts; the desktop app loads nothing from the internet and uses your computer’s fonts.

Questions and fixes

My drawing disappeared.

Drawings are saved in the browser you used, so check you are in the same browser (and not a private window). If you cleared your browsing data, the drawing is gone, which is why Save › Project file is worth doing for anything you care about.

The Add to drawing button is grey.

The code has a problem or draws nothing. Read the message under the editor; the line with the problem has a red number.

Can I use what I make commercially?

Yes. Your drawings are yours. The app itself is MIT-licensed open source.

Keyboard shortcuts do nothing.

A text field probably has focus (for example, the code editor). Press Esc to leave it, or click the canvas.

The desktop app won’t open on my Mac.

Open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway next to the message about artful drawing. You only need to do this once.

How do I move my saved instructions to another computer?

In the Code tab, open Edit My instructions and click Download. On the other computer, paste the file’s text into the same box.

Something looks wrong or broken.

Please open an issue on GitHub with a screenshot and what you clicked. Saving a project file and attaching it helps a lot.

Nothing in the manual matches that. Try fewer letters, or ask the studio’s command palette (Ctrl+K).
